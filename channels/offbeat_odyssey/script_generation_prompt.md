Write a 1,400-1,700 word narrated Top 10 travel list video script about [TOPIC]. This is an enthusiastic countdown told by a well-traveled narrator who genuinely loves sharing incredible places. The topic may be about a specific US destination OR a comparative list across multiple destinations (e.g., "Top 10 most stunning small towns in America"). Adapt accordingly — for aggregate topics, each item should be a different destination with enough detail to make the viewer want to visit.

RETENTION STRUCTURE — every section must keep the viewer watching through all 10 items:

1. COLD OPEN — THE HOOK (first 3-4 sentences, ~30 seconds):
Drop the viewer into the most stunning or surprising fact about this destination. NOT a generic intro. Example: "Savannah, Georgia has been called the most beautiful city in North America. With its moss-draped oaks, secret gardens, and a history that reads like a novel — it's easy to see why. But most visitors only scratch the surface." Then plant an OPEN LOOP: "I'm counting down the 10 most stunning places in Savannah — and number one? It's a spot most tourists walk right past."

2. ITEMS 10 THROUGH 6 — THE WARM-UP (~500-600 words):
Start with great picks that build momentum. For each item:
- Name the specific place, trail, experience, or attraction
- Describe what makes it special with vivid sensory details
- Include one surprising fact, practical tip, or local secret
- Transition with energy: "But that's just the beginning. Number 7 is going to blow your mind."
Keep each item to 80-120 words. These are strong picks but you're saving the best for the top 5.

3. ITEMS 5 THROUGH 2 — THE HEAVY HITTERS (~500-600 words):
These are the destinations that make people book a trip. For each item:
- Open with why this one ranks higher — what sets it apart
- Paint a vivid picture: what you'd see, hear, and feel being there
- Layer in history, local culture, or a story that brings it to life
- Use re-hooks between items: "And if you thought that was incredible, wait until you see number 3."
Each item gets 100-150 words. Build anticipation for number 1.

4. NUMBER 1 — THE SHOWSTOPPER (~150-200 words):
This is the moment the viewer has been waiting for. Build it up: "And the number one spot? This is the one that keeps people coming back year after year." Give it the most detail, the best storytelling, and the most vivid description. Explain why it earned the top spot. Make the viewer feel like they're standing there.

5. THE CLOSING — INSPIRATION, NOT A LECTURE (~60-80 words):
End with genuine enthusiasm about the destination as a whole. NOT a call to action, NOT "what do you think?" — just a warm, inspiring final thought that makes the viewer want to start planning a trip. Something like: "Whether you're chasing history, nature, or just a place that takes your breath away — this is one of those destinations that stays with you long after you leave."

PACING & RETENTION TECHNIQUES:
- Use OPEN LOOPS — tease higher-ranked items before you get there. "We haven't even gotten to the spot where you can see three states at once."
- RE-HOOK between every 2-3 items. Drop a line that creates anticipation: "But the top 5? That's where it gets really incredible."
- VARY THE PACING — short, punchy sentences for dramatic reveals. Longer, flowing sentences for scenic descriptions.
- SENSORY DETAILS — don't just list attractions. Describe the morning mist over the mountains, the sound of waves crashing against the cliffs, the smell of fresh biscuits from that famous bakery.
- CONVERSATIONAL TONE — write like an excited friend sharing travel tips. Natural speech, contractions, occasional rhetorical questions ("Can you even imagine waking up to this view?").
- PRACTICAL VALUE — weave in tips: best time to visit, free vs. paid, insider tricks, best viewpoints. This keeps it useful, not just entertaining.
- NO FILLER — every sentence must either inform, amaze, or build anticipation. Cut anything generic.

FACTS & ACCURACY:
- Use real, verifiable facts about each location
- Include specific details: distances, heights, dates, history
- Name real places, trails, restaurants, viewpoints, and landmarks
- If there's a famous local story or legend, include it
- Do NOT fabricate attractions or statistics
