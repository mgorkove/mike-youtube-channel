Write a 1,400-1,700 word narrated travel video script about [TOPIC]. This is a curious, wonder-filled exploration of an unusual destination, told by a well-traveled narrator who genuinely loves discovering strange and surprising places.

RETENTION STRUCTURE — every section must make the viewer feel they CANNOT stop watching:

1. COLD OPEN — THE HOOK (first 3-4 sentences, ~30 seconds):
Drop the viewer into the most jaw-dropping fact or visual about this place. NOT a generic intro. Example: "There's a cave in Vietnam so enormous that it has its own jungle, its own river, and its own weather system. Clouds form inside it. And until 2009, nobody on Earth even knew it existed." Then plant an OPEN LOOP: "But getting there? That's a story in itself. Let me take you inside."

2. THE DISCOVERY — HOW THIS PLACE EXISTS (~200-250 words):
How was this place discovered, built, or formed? What's the origin story? Use one or two hyper-specific details that make it feel real and grounded. Geological history, cultural context, or the story of whoever found it. End with a hook to the next section: "But that's just the surface. What makes this place truly bizarre is what happens when you actually go there."

3. THE DEEP DIVE — WHAT MAKES IT EXTRAORDINARY (~400-500 words):
This is the meat of the video. Take the viewer on a virtual tour. Describe what you'd see, hear, smell, and feel if you were actually there. Layer in surprising facts, local legends, and historical context. Use the "just when you think you've seen it all" technique — reveal a new layer that makes it even more incredible than expected. Key retention moves:
- After one amazing detail: "And that's not even the strangest part."
- After a bigger revelation: "But here's where it gets really wild."
- Plant what's coming: "There's one thing about this place that most visitors never discover."

4. THE PEOPLE & CULTURE (~200-250 words):
Who lives here or visits? What's daily life like? Are there local traditions, legends, or controversies tied to this place? This humanizes the destination and gives viewers an emotional connection. Include dialogue or anecdotes if possible.

5. THE TWIST OR HIDDEN LAYER (~150-200 words):
Every great travel story has a twist. Maybe this place is threatened by climate change, or there's a dark history beneath the beauty, or visiting is about to become impossible. This adds depth and urgency. "What most people don't realize is..."

6. THE CLOSING — WONDER, NOT A LECTURE (~80-120 words):
End on a note of awe and curiosity. NOT a call to action, NOT "what do you think?" — just a beautifully crafted final thought that makes the viewer sit with the wonder of what they just learned. The last line should linger. Something like: "Sometimes the most extraordinary places on Earth are the ones we almost never find."

PACING & RETENTION TECHNIQUES:
- Use OPEN LOOPS constantly — tease something before you deliver it. "What they found at the bottom changed everything." Then don't reveal it for 2-3 sentences.
- RE-HOOK every 60-90 seconds (~150-225 words). Drop a line that makes the viewer think "wait, really?" Examples: "But that's not even the most incredible part." / "And then there's the thing nobody talks about." / "What happened next shocked even the scientists."
- VARY THE PACING — short, punchy sentences for dramatic reveals. Longer, flowing sentences for scenic descriptions. Never let the rhythm become monotonous.
- SENSORY DETAILS — don't just list facts. Describe the mist rising off the water at dawn, the echo inside a cathedral-sized cave, the smell of sulfur near a volcanic vent.
- CONVERSATIONAL TONE — write like an excited friend sharing a discovery over drinks. Natural speech, contractions, occasional rhetorical questions ("Can you imagine standing there?").
- NO FILLER — every sentence must either inform, amaze, or build tension. Cut anything that doesn't serve one of those purposes.

FACTS & ACCURACY:
- Use real, verifiable facts about the destination
- Include specific numbers: dimensions, distances, populations, dates
- Name real geographic features, nearby cities, and countries
- If there are competing theories or legends, present them honestly
- Do NOT fabricate historical events or statistics
