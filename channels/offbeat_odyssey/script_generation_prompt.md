Write a 1,400-1,700 word narrated video script about [TOPIC]. This is a deep-dive into a single unusual, bizarre, or stunning destination — told by a curious, well-traveled narrator who can't believe this place actually exists.

RETENTION STRUCTURE — every section must keep the viewer watching to the end:

1. COLD OPEN — THE HOOK (first 3-4 sentences, ~30 seconds):
Drop the viewer into the single most unbelievable fact about this place. NOT a generic intro. Example: "There's a lake in Senegal that turns bright pink. Not a filter. Not a trick. It's called Lac Rose, and every year, it transforms into something that looks like it was poured out of a flamingo." Then plant an OPEN LOOP: "But the color isn't even the strangest part. Wait until you hear what's at the bottom."

2. THE REVEAL — WHAT IS THIS PLACE? (~300-400 words):
Now explain what the place actually is. Where is it? What does it look like? What's it called? Give the viewer the full picture with vivid sensory details — what you'd see, hear, and feel standing there. Include specific geography: country, region, nearest city, how remote it is. Make the viewer feel like they're arriving for the first time.

3. THE STORY — HOW DID THIS HAPPEN? (~400-500 words):
Every bizarre place has a story. How did it get this way? Was it natural? Man-made? An accident? Go deep into the history, geology, culture, or legend behind this place. Layer in specific facts: dates, measurements, population numbers, scientific explanations. This is where you earn the viewer's trust by showing you actually researched this. Use re-hooks: "But that's not even the most incredible part."

4. THE DETAILS — WHAT MOST PEOPLE DON'T KNOW (~300-400 words):
Now go beyond the surface. What do most articles and videos miss? Include 2-3 surprising layers: local customs, controversies, access restrictions, what it's like to actually visit, how locals feel about tourists, preservation efforts, or recent changes. Weave in practical details: can you visit? How do you get there? What does it cost? Is it dangerous?

5. THE CLOSING — LEAVE THEM THINKING (~60-80 words):
End with a genuine reflection on what makes this place special in the bigger picture. NOT a call to action, NOT "what do you think?" — just a thought-provoking final observation. Something like: "In a world where every corner seems mapped and photographed, places like this remind you that the planet still has secrets. And some of them are hiding in plain sight."

PACING & RETENTION TECHNIQUES:
- Use OPEN LOOPS — tease later reveals before you get there. "We haven't even gotten to why the government tried to seal this place shut."
- RE-HOOK every 2-3 minutes. Drop a line that creates anticipation: "But the real story? That's coming."
- VARY THE PACING — short, punchy sentences for shocking reveals. Longer, flowing sentences for scenic descriptions.
- SENSORY DETAILS — don't just state facts. Describe the turquoise water crashing against jet-black volcanic rock, the echo inside a cavern so large it has its own weather system, the silence of a town where no cars have driven in a hundred years.
- CONVERSATIONAL TONE — write like an amazed friend, not a textbook. Natural speech, contractions, genuine wonder. "Seriously, how is this real?"
- SPECIFICITY — always use real names, real numbers, real distances. "The crystals are up to 12 meters long" beats "the crystals are enormous."
- NO FILLER — every sentence must either inform, amaze, or build anticipation. Cut anything generic.

FACTS & ACCURACY:
- Use real, verifiable facts about the location
- Include specific details: distances, heights, dates, populations, temperatures
- Name real places, landmarks, geographic features, and historical figures
- If there's a famous local story, legend, or controversy, include it
- Do NOT fabricate facts, statistics, or historical events
- If something is disputed or uncertain, say so — "some historians believe" is better than stating fiction as fact
