Write a 3,500-4,500 word educational narration script about [TOPIC]. This is an informative health and wellness video for adults over 50, presented in a warm, authoritative, and conversational tone.

RETENTION STRUCTURE — every section must keep the viewer engaged and watching:

1. COLD OPEN — THE HOOK (first 3-4 sentences, ~30 seconds):
Start with a bold, surprising claim or little-known fact that immediately challenges common assumptions. Plant an OPEN LOOP that makes the viewer need to keep watching. Example: "There's an herb growing in backyards across America right now that researchers say could be more powerful than most over-the-counter anti-inflammatories. And chances are, you've been pulling it out of the ground and throwing it away. But before I tell you what it is, you need to understand why this matters — especially after 60." Then tease what's coming: "Stick around because number 14 on this list is something doctors are just now starting to recommend."

2. THE FOUNDATION — WHY THIS MATTERS (~200-300 words):
Briefly establish why this topic is important, especially for people over 50. Reference real health concerns (inflammation, joint pain, cognitive decline, heart health, gut health, sleep issues) that the audience actually deals with. Connect the topic to ancestral or traditional wisdom — people trust knowledge that has stood the test of time. End with a forward hook: "And that brings us to the first one on our list — and it might be the most surprising of all."

3. THE LIST — MAIN CONTENT (~2,800-3,500 words):
Present each item with this structure:
- NAME and quick identification (what it is, where it comes from)
- HISTORICAL/TRADITIONAL USE — how ancient cultures or traditional medicine used it (Ayurveda, TCM, Native American, European folk medicine, etc.)
- MODERN SCIENCE — what research says about it today (cite specific compounds, mechanisms, or study findings without getting overly technical)
- PRACTICAL APPLICATION — how the viewer can actually use it (tea, tincture, supplement, grow at home, add to food)
- One specific, memorable detail that makes this item stick in the viewer's mind

Between items, use TRANSITION HOOKS to maintain momentum:
- "But this next one might surprise you even more..."
- "Now, if you think that was impressive, wait until you hear about number 12..."
- "This next herb has been hiding in plain sight for centuries..."
- "And here's where things get really interesting..."

4. THE CALLBACK — TYING IT TOGETHER (~150-200 words):
Brief wrap-up that connects the items back to the bigger picture — taking control of your health naturally, rediscovering what our ancestors knew, simple changes that make a real difference. End on an empowering note — NOT a sales pitch, NOT "like and subscribe," just genuine encouragement.

PACING & RETENTION TECHNIQUES:
- RE-HOOK every 90-120 seconds (~225-300 words). Drop a teaser for what's coming. Examples: "But the next one on our list has a compound so powerful that pharmaceutical companies have been trying to synthesize it for decades." / "And number 15 — I saved this one because it might be the most important herb on this entire list."
- OPEN LOOPS — tease items before you get to them. "We'll get to the one herb that outperformed a leading prescription drug in a clinical trial — but first..."
- VARY THE PACING — mix shorter punchy entries (100-150 words) with deeper dives (250-350 words) on the most interesting items. Don't make every entry the same length.
- SPECIFICITY BUILDS TRUST — use specific compound names (curcumin, allicin, rosmarinic acid), specific traditional uses ("used by Okinawan healers for centuries"), and specific mechanisms ("blocks the COX-2 inflammatory pathway"). But explain each one simply — never assume the viewer has a science background.
- CONVERSATIONAL TONE — write like a knowledgeable friend explaining things over coffee. Natural speech patterns, contractions, occasional rhetorical questions ("Sound familiar?", "Ever wonder why your grandmother swore by this?"). Warm and encouraging, never condescending.
- SENSORY DETAILS — describe how herbs look, smell, taste. "The bright golden color of turmeric root..." "That sharp, almost peppery scent when you crush fresh rosemary between your fingers..."

CRITICAL RULES:
- NEVER give specific medical dosages or claim to cure/treat/prevent any disease
- Use language like "may support," "research suggests," "traditionally used for," "studies indicate"
- NEVER moralize or lecture — inform and empower
- NEVER use "guys" or overly casual language — the audience is mature adults
- NEVER reference subscribing, liking, notifications, or any YouTube mechanics
- DO NOT use clickbait language in the script itself — save drama for titles, keep the script credible and trustworthy
- Include a mix of well-known items (turmeric, garlic) and surprising/lesser-known ones (schisandra, ashitaba, andrographis) — the lesser-known ones drive the most engagement
- Each item should feel like a mini-revelation, not a textbook entry
