Write a 750-850 word YouTube script about [TOPIC]. This is a second-person immersive narration that walks the viewer through EVERY RANK in the organization, from the absolute lowest to the highest. Target video length is 5 minutes. The format is inspired by "Your Life as Every [X] Rank" videos.

VOICE & TONE:
- Second person, present tense throughout: "You're 18. You just stepped off the bus." NOT past tense. NOT third person.
- Cold, matter-of-fact delivery. NOT preachy, NOT patriotic, NOT motivational. Just raw, honest, vivid.
- Short, punchy sentences. Many sentences should be 3-8 words. "Cash. No questions." / "You don't sleep." / "That's your squad now."
- Mix terse declarations with occasional longer descriptive sentences for rhythm.
- Use SPECIFIC numbers everywhere: exact pay grades, hours awake, miles run, team sizes, casualty rates, years of service.
- Sensory details that put the viewer IN the scene: what you hear, see, feel, smell.
- No narrator judgment or moral commentary. Just show the reality. Let the viewer draw conclusions.
- State the character's age at key moments to show a life progressing: "You're 18... You're 24... You're 32... You're 45..."

STRUCTURE — follow this exact format:

1. NO INTRO. NO PREAMBLE. Start immediately with the first rank.

2. RANK-BY-RANK PROGRESSION (the core ~670-780 words):
Open EVERY rank section with: "Level [number], the [Rank Title]."

For each rank/level:
- Immediately place the viewer in a specific scene with vivid sensory details
- Describe daily life at this rank — duties, routines, what you're responsible for
- Include at least ONE concrete, specific moment that brings this rank to life
- State the pay/compensation (exact figures ground the experience in reality)
- Show what changed from the previous rank — more authority, more danger, more isolation, more politics
- For combat ranks: describe operational reality — missions, risks, what failure looks like
- For higher ranks: shift to leadership burden, strategic decisions, political dimensions, loneliness of command
- End each rank section with a hook or transition that pulls the viewer to the next level

Aim for 7-10 levels per video. Spend roughly equal word count on each, but the middle ranks (peak action/operational) should feel the most intense and detailed. Compress very similar adjacent ranks if needed.

The progression should feel like a LIFE being lived. Early ranks = physical grind, exhaustion, being broken down and rebuilt. Middle ranks = operational excellence, peak danger, brotherhood. Top ranks = strategic weight, isolation, the cost of command.

3. CLOSING — THE CYCLE (2-3 sentences, ~40 words):
End with a brief moment showing the cycle beginning again — a new recruit arriving, a kid signing up, someone stepping off a bus just like you did. This creates a haunting, cyclical ending. Do NOT ask the viewer to subscribe or like. Just end.

PACING:
- Relentless. No filler. No tangents. Every sentence advances the story or deepens the immersion.
- The "Level X" announcements serve as natural section breaks that keep the viewer oriented and create a "next level" pull.
- Re-hook the viewer every 100-150 words with a vivid detail, a dramatic shift, or a short punchy line: "And then everything changes." / "This is where most people break." / "Nobody told you about this part."
- Escalate the stakes with each level. Power, danger, responsibility, and cost all increase.

VISUAL SCENE GUIDANCE:
Each rank section must describe a visually distinct setting or action — these will be illustrated as cartoon scenes. Think about:
- Different locations: bus stops, barracks, training grounds, jungle, desert, ocean, briefing rooms, command centers, mess halls
- Different gear: basic fatigues, specialized equipment, dress uniforms, tactical loadouts
- Different body language: exhausted recruits, focused operators, commanding officers standing alone
- Different lighting/atmosphere: predawn runs, night operations, fluorescent briefing rooms, dusty forward bases
- The main character should be visually present in most scenes — show them progressing through each rank

FACTUAL ACCURACY:
- Use real rank titles in the correct order for the specific organization
- Use accurate (or close-to-accurate) pay figures, training durations, and unit structures
- If covering a historical organization (Spartans, Roman Legions, Samurai), use historically grounded details but present them in the same immersive second-person style
- Do NOT invent fictional operations or classified details — stick to publicly known information presented dramatically

WHAT TO AVOID:
- No first person ("I", "me", "my") — this is entirely second-person
- No meta-commentary about the video itself ("in this video", "today we'll explore")
- No calls to action (subscribe, like, comment)
- No filler phrases ("without further ado", "let's dive in", "buckle up")
- No generic motivational language ("heroes", "brave warriors", "ultimate sacrifice" used generically)
- No political opinions about wars or conflicts
- No glorification of violence — describe the reality honestly, including the cost
- No long complex sentences. Keep it punchy.
